# Research-gap register — tender-db, 2026-08-09

**Method note.** I read SUMMARY.md in full (§1 index, §2 register, §4 closure, §5 decisions, §6 drift), all 15 research docs' claims/open-question sections, CONTEXT.md, docs/architecture.md, all 9 ADRs (via summaries and cross-refs), the full issue board (146 files, headers + deep reads of 23, 46, 120, and the data-quality/pilot-sizing docs), and the 2026-08-07 handover. This is an internal gap analysis; no web research done. Bottom line up front: the corpus is unusually complete on **sources and formats** (TED channels, eForms EU/DE, DÖE, legacy mapping, identifier empirics) and genuinely thin in four places: **the public surface under load, organization identity, the storage/DR lifecycle at current scale, and the data's value domains**. I found 8 real gaps, 3 of them pre-launch.

## 4. Ranked gap register

### Gap 1 — Capacity and abuse model for the public surface *(now / hard pre-launch)*
**Unknown:** what one 4-vCPU box actually sustains: worst-case cost per REST endpoint, SSE fan-out cost (per-change predicate evaluation × N subscriptions, snapshot cost under the 5/IP anonymous cap), SQL-endpoint saturation behavior, and importer-vs-API contention — given the measured fact (issue 120) that **no query can be stopped once started** and 8 reader connections are the entire concurrency budget.
**Why it matters:** this is the strongest pivot risk in the system. ADR-0005's monolith plus turso's single-process constraint mean you *cannot* bolt on a second reader process later; if launch traffic or one hostile SQL user saturates the box, the fixes available are per-query hacks (the current pattern) or an architecture pivot (own runtime → own process → different engine). The api-layer research covered mechanisms (rate limiter, token storage, gate layers), never capacity; its one load-bearing runtime claim (timeout-by-drop) proved false.
**Study:** a measured load/abuse campaign against a prod-shaped copy (needs the owner conversation prod-box-reads.md mandates, since heavy reads have no on-box path): per-endpoint worst-case latency/CPU, SSE cost curve vs subscription count and change rate, concurrent-hostile-SQL behavior, and from it a written capacity budget that the rate-limit numbers and issue-120's design can be derived from instead of posture. Days, not weeks.
**When:** before launch. Issues 120/17/163 track fixes; none of them supplies the numbers.

### Gap 2 — Organization identifier landscape *(before launch, if Organizations are a headline feature)*
**Unknown:** what official-identifier schemes actually appear across ~30 countries and 30 years in the corpus: scheme attributes and their absence, national register formats (DE HRB/HRA/VR, FR SIREN/SIRET, and 25 more), VAT formats, how often the same string means different entities across countries, and the real false-merge/false-split rates of the current exact-match auto-merge.
**Why it matters:** CONTEXT promises "one profile per real-world entity" as a *product feature*. The corpus has one paragraph of evidence (legacy fill rates). Issue 86 shows the cost of format naivety already: a German register prefix read as a country code, NULL-id duplicate profiles. Under-merge degrades the feature; over-merge (two entities sharing a junk id like "0000") corrupts it publicly. Mentions are immutable so recovery is re-projection, not data loss — a hack, not a pivot — but the feature's credibility at launch rides on it.
**Study:** empirical, from the product's own SQL surface (bounded reads): distribution of identifier schemes/formats per country per era, collision analysis of current merge keys, a normalization + plausibility rule set with measured effect. One to two days plus the B8 design it feeds.

### Gap 3 — Storage lifecycle at 0.5 TB and beyond *(now)*
**Unknown:** measured DB growth rate at steady state + rebuild cadence; turso behavior in the 0.5–1 TB range (the bench stopped at 10 GB; both turso-scale and pilot-sizing flagged the bigger run as needed research and it never ran); and above all the **compaction dead end**: VACUUM INTO is verified to OOM, so a turso DB can never shrink — while the change log is promised "forever", rebuilds append full-corpus change rows, and `/data` (1 TB, DB + archive together) is already **~87 % full**.
**Why it matters:** the disk-arithmetic research (pilot-sizing, A2) was excellent but ended at "buy a volume"; nobody has re-done the arithmetic at post-backfill reality. If growth eats the ~130 GB headroom, the only escape is dump-and-reload of half a terabyte (wall-clock never measured, service down throughout) onto a bigger volume — an ops crisis that is entirely predictable today.
**Study:** compute the actual growth curve from `fetches`/`notices`/`changes` timestamps (cheap, metadata-only); measure a dump-and-reload on scratch hardware; re-run the scale suite at 40–100 GB as originally planned; produce a dated capacity model with a "volume full" date and a change-log retention recommendation (C17's reserved pruning path needs a trigger criterion). D12 monitoring exists; the *model* doesn't.

### Gap 4 — DR premise and unrebuildable user state *(before launch)*
**Unknown:** true end-to-end recovery time from archive at current scale (the decision's "roughly a day" traces to a measurement that excluded projection); and the fate of the state that is *not* derivable from sources — accounts, tokens, webhook registrations, cursor/epoch continuity — after the 2026-08-06 removal of snapshots.
**Why it matters:** pre-launch, losing the volume costs days of compute. Post-launch it additionally destroys every user account and subscriber integration with no recovery path — a different decision than the one Lennart signed off, which predates users. Being wrong forces an emergency backup retrofit under incident conditions.
**Study:** cheap — one honest paper estimate of full re-ingest+project from measured stage rates (or a scratch drill), an enumeration of non-derivable tables, and a design note for a tiny off-box copy of just the user-state tables (MBs, not the 500 GB problem issue 23 stalled on). Re-decide "no backups" with the real numbers on the table.

### Gap 5 — The change-feed contract across rebuilds *(before launch; mostly board-tracked)*
**Unknown:** the unified externally-visible contract: what a subscriber (SSE, poll, webhook) observes across a routine ADR-0009 rebuild, a retirement (issue 164: `removed` rows invisible to SSE), and a cursor reset — as one documented protocol rather than three patches.
**Why it matters:** this is the one surface where post-launch changes are breaking changes for external integrations; issue 46's own recommendation ("one final clean rebuild / epoch start before launch") has a use-by date.
**Honesty note:** the board covers most of this (46 ready-for-agent, 164 open, 163 open); the genuine *research* residue is small — settle the epoch/reset semantics once, write it into the API docs, and add a replaying-client conformance test. I list it because SUMMARY §4 still calls B1 "resolved design" while its premise is dead, and no doc supersedes it.

### Gap 6 — Value-domain profile of the data itself *(before launch; cheap)*
**Unknown:** the actual distributions of amounts (negatives, sub-cent, absurd magnitudes), currencies (incl. pre-euro), dates (impossible deadlines, timezone artifacts), and coded values per era — the research corpus profiled structure and identifiers exhaustively and content values not at all; the data-quality tool measures field *presence*, not *validity*.
**Why it matters:** every value pathology so far was discovered by accident in production and cost an investigation chain (issues 131→136 alone are six documents). Public SQL users will find the rest and judge the "honest data" promise by it. Wrong here forces no pivot — but it forces the current pattern forever: one surprise, one multi-agent forensic loop.
**Study:** one systematic bounded-SQL profiling pass per era over the numeric/date/code satellites, yielding (a) a validation-rule catalog for ingestion, (b) a public "known data caveats" doc. Two days; the 10 s/single-SELECT constraints already proved sufficient for exactly this shape of work (issues 136/137).

### Gap 7 — Classification and currency drift across 30 years *(conditional: before promoting cross-era analytics)*
**Unknown:** NUTS revisions (multiple within the XML era alone) mean region codes silently change meaning across years; text-era notices predate CPV-2008; pre-1999 amounts are in national currencies; C10 (EUR-at-date, rate source) was never decided *or* researched. Zero corpus coverage (the grep is empty).
**Why it matters:** the product promise is "market analysis… across historical and live data". A user summing a NUTS region or a CPV prefix across 2011–2026 gets silently wrong answers at every vocabulary boundary. Fix-if-wrong is a mapping-table layer plus derived columns — a bounded retrofit, not a pivot — which is why this ranks below the others.
**Study:** enumerate which codelist versions actually appear per era (empirical, from own DB), map the deltas, decide C10. Needed only when analytics is marketed; a caveats paragraph is the interim honest fix.

### Gap 8 — Redaction/takedown path *(mechanism: conditional; the record half is closed)*
**Unknown:** whether sources ever retract or redact published notices. D4's re-hash probes and D5's reveal recheck were implemented as weekly scheduled jobs on 2026-08-23 (issue 173) — the measurement now accumulates. (The former half of this gap, the legal-assessment record, closed 2026-08-23: Lennart confirmed directly that the lawyer assessed all stored information as public; issue 173 carries the dated note.)
**Why it matters:** if a takedown obligation ever arrives (source-side redaction, a court order), there is no mechanism to remove one notice from an append-only versioned store, its archive tar, and its change history. That retrofit is the definition of the hack the goal's bar names. Low probability; asymmetric cost; the cheap insurance is a tombstone design note, not machinery.

**Conditional (only if X):** EU-level expansion beyond Germany has no research (german-portals is DE-only; service.bund.de research is in flight in this session). ADR-0003's merge was validated on one UUID-exact pair (DÖE↔TED); most national portals won't offer that, so per-source cross-reference research is needed *per new source* — but the island-Tender fallback means nothing breaks meanwhile. Not a present gap.
